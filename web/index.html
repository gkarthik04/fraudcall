How it works

From dataset transcripts to a streaming fraud score, all on CPU.

01

Data

800 balanced conversations from ICFD-31k (Roman-script English and Hinglish), split by session_id into 557 train, 119 validation and 124 test calls.

02

Synthetic calls

Piper text-to-speech reads each conversation with a pool of voices. A second system (MMS-TTS) renders an unseen test set. No real voice is cloned.

03

Phone line

8 kHz, G.711, GSM, AMR-NB and Opus codecs, white, pink and brown noise at set SNRs, and 20 ms packet loss: 14 test conditions.

04

Recognition

faster-whisper (base, int8) transcribes the degraded call once; the transcript is cut at each 10-second step.

05

Models

BiLSTM on recognised text (1.5 M parameters), CNN-BiGRU on the last 30 s of log-mel audio (0.11 M) and an early-fusion model (1.7 M).

06

Alert

The alert threshold is the score that only 5 % of normal validation calls ever reach. Time-to-detection is the first step above it.

Limits worth knowing

  • All audio is synthetic speech generated from dataset transcripts, so results may not transfer to real calls.
  • The audio branch was near chance and fusion matched text-only: the injected acoustic cue was weak.
  • English and Hinglish only. Telugu and Devanagari Hindi were not tested; the transformer baseline was not run.
